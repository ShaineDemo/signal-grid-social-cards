# Narrative system

## Compression ladder

Reduce the source in this order:

1. Verified event: what happened, with date/version when relevant.
2. User change: what the audience can now do or experience.
3. Access gate: model, hardware, region, subscription, staged rollout.
4. Meaning: why this changes the product or market.
5. Boundary: what is not confirmed, not available, or still limited.
6. Takeaway: what the reader should check, try, or remember.

## Default six-card arc

```text
01 Hook       — concrete event + emotional verb
02 Change     — version/date and the before→after shift
03 Capability — 3–4 sourced actions or experiences
04 Access     — who gets it, how to open it, required conditions
05 Meaning    — interpretation clearly separated from reported fact
06 Boundary   — limits, staged availability, and final takeaway
```

Use a different arc when the source does not support six meaningful ideas. Five strong cards are better than six padded ones.

## Claim discipline

Classify every material statement:

- **Confirmed:** directly supported by an official source.
- **Reported:** supported by a reputable report but not the primary source.
- **Inference:** a reasoned interpretation; write it as interpretation.
- **Unknown:** conflicting or unconfirmed; omit or name the uncertainty.

Never place an inference inside a giant numeral or definitive cover headline.

## Copy patterns

Preferred:

- “车机开始会聊天了”
- “2026.14.13 / 分批推送”
- “能聊、能查、能讲故事”
- “先看你的车是否收到更新”
- “这次补的是座舱交互，不等于自动驾驶升级”

Avoid:

- “史诗级”“彻底颠覆”“遥遥领先” without evidence.
- Dense corporate chronology on the cover.
- Treating “接入模型” as proof of every theoretical model capability.
- Mixing an AI assistant update with FSD/autonomous-driving claims.
